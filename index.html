1. Definitions & Conceptual Foundations

Core political theory principles according to classic scholars

Theory

Woolsey

"A constitution is the collection of principles according to which the power of government, the rights of the governed, and the relation between the two are adjusted."

Core Key: Government Power vs Citizen Rights

Lord Bryce

"A constitution is a set of established rules embodying and enacting the practice of government."

Core Key: Established Rules & Government Practice

Gilchrist

"A constitution is the body of rules or laws, written or unwritten, determining the organization of government, distribution of powers, and general principles of exercise."

Core Key: Distribution & Exercise of Power

Four Major Pillars Established by a Constitution

  1. 1 Organization & Powers: Structure of executive, legislature, and judiciary organs.
  2. 2 Political Process: Principles and rules governing national political participation and elections.
  3. 3 People-State Relations: Defining the authority limits and administrative boundaries.
  4. 4 Rights & Duties: Protecting fundamental rights and legal duties of citizens.

Types of Constitutions

Written vs Unwritten Set down in a book vs uncodified traditions. BD is Written.
Flexible vs Rigid Easily amended vs $2/3$ majority required. BD is Rigid.
Evolved vs Enacted Slow historical growth vs created by Constituent Assembly. BD is Enacted.
Enacted Examples India, USA, Japan, China, and Bangladesh.

10 Essentials of a Good Constitution

1. Clear Form: Simple, unambiguous language.
2. Legal Supremacy: Enjoys supremacy over ordinary laws.
3. Adaptability: Balanced rigidity and flexibility.
4. Fundamental Rights: Guarantees civil liberties.
5. Clear Organs: Defines Executive, Legislature, Judiciary.
6. Rule of Law: Equal protection under law.
7. Decentralization: Power distributed to local bodies.
8. Judicial Independence: Powerful & impartial courts.
9. Sound Amendments: Structured procedure (Art 142).
10. Free Elections: Independent electoral machinery.

3. High-Yield Articles & Rights Matrix

Detailed categorization of the 18 Fundamental Rights in Part III

Part III
Article 27 Group A (Absolute)
Equality Before Law

All citizens are equal before law and are entitled to equal protection of law.

Article 28 Group A (Absolute)
Non-Discrimination

No discrimination against any citizen on grounds only of religion, race, caste, sex, or place of birth.

Article 29 Group A (Absolute)
Equality of Public Employment

Equal opportunity for all citizens in respect of employment or office in the service of Republic.

Article 30 Group A (Absolute)
Prohibition of Foreign Titles

No citizen shall accept any title, honour, award, or decoration from any foreign state without prior approval.

Article 33 Group A (Absolute)
Safeguards as to Arrest/Detention

Right to be informed of grounds of arrest, right to consult lawyer, and production before Magistrate within 24 hours.

Article 34 Group A (Absolute)
Prohibition of Forced Labour

All forms of forced labour are prohibited and punishable by law.

Article 35 Group A (Absolute)
Protection in Trial & Punishment

Protection against ex-post facto laws, double jeopardy, and self-incrimination.

Article 44 Group A (Absolute)
Enforcement of Fundamental Rights

Right to move High Court Division under Article 102 for enforcement of Part III rights.

Article 36 Group B (Restricted)
Freedom of Movement

Subject to reasonable restrictions imposed by law in the public interest.

Article 37 Group B (Restricted)
Freedom of Assembly

Right to assemble peacefully and without arms, subject to reasonable restrictions.

Article 38 Group B (Restricted)
Freedom of Association

Right to form associations or unions, subject to reasonable restrictions in public interest.

Article 39 Group B (Restricted)
Thought, Speech & Press

Freedom of thought, conscience, speech, expression, and press freedom subject to security restrictions.

Article 41 Group B (Restricted)
Freedom of Religion

Right to profess, practice or propagate any religion, subject to law and public order.

Article 43 Group B (Restricted)
Protection of Home & Mail

Privacy of home and correspondence protected, subject to legal search restrictions.

Article 31 Group C (Legislative)
Right to Protection of Law

Inalienable right to enjoy protection of law and be treated in accordance with law.

Article 32 Group C (Legislative)
Protection of Life & Liberty

No person shall be deprived of life or personal liberty save in accordance with law.

Article 40 Group C (Legislative)
Lawful Profession or Trade

Right to enter upon any lawful profession or occupation, subject to qualifications prescribed by law.

Article 42 Group C (Legislative)
Protection of Property Rights

Right to acquire, hold, transfer or dispose of property, subject to conditions by law.

Special Case: Is Education a Fundamental Right?

Education is a Directive Principle (Art. 17)

No, education is NOT listed under Part III Fundamental Rights. Instead, Article 17 establishes universal, free, and compulsory education as a Directive Principle of State Policy.

Global Contrast: Universal Declaration of Human Rights (UDHR 1948) explicitly recognizes education as a universal right.
Voting Rights & Sovereignty

Articles 7(1), 11 & 122

Citizens are supreme power holders (Art 7). Under Article 122, every citizen of sound mind who is 18 years or older has the right to vote in national and local elections, irrespective of caste, religion, or social status.

Applies to National, District & Local Body Elections

5. Amendment Mechanics (Art 142) & Major Amendments

17 Amendments made to date; key structural shifts in governance

Article 142

How to Change the Constitution (Article 142 Flow)

Step 1 Clarity of changed material stated in Bill
Step 2 2/3 Votes of total Parliament members
Step 3 President's assent & formal approval
4th Amendment 25 Jan 1975
Presidential System & One-Party

Replaced Parliamentary system with Presidential system; instituted one-party rule, curtailed JS power, and restricted Supreme Court jurisdiction over fundamental rights enforcement.

8th Amendment 7 June 1988
State Religion & High Court Benches

Declared Islam state religion; set 6 High Court benches outside Dhaka; changed 'Bengali'→'Bangla' and 'Dacca'→'Dhaka'. (SC later struck down regional HC benches as basic structure violation).

12th Amendment 6 Aug 1991
Restored Parliamentary System

Major landmark: Restored Westminster parliamentary democracy; President became constitutional head and PM executive head. Vice-President post abolished.

14th Amendment 16 May 2004
45 Women Seats & Retirement Ages

Reserved 45 women's seats in JS; raised retirement age of SC judges (65→67), PSC Chair (62→65), and CAG (60→65); displayed presidential portraits.

15th Amendment 30 June 2011
Caretaker Abolished & 50 Women Seats

Abolished caretaker government; inserted Articles 7A & 7B (basic features non-amendable); restored Secularism (Art 12); expanded reserved women seats to 50; citizens termed 'Bangalees' nationally.

16th Amendment 17 Sept 2014
Judicial Impeachment Power

Empowered Parliament to remove SC judges for proved misbehaviour/incapacity by 2/3 vote (Art 96).

Judicial Note: High Court (May 2016) & Appellate Division (July 2017) declared 16th Amendment unconstitutional, restoring Supreme Judicial Council.

6. Constitutional Shortcomings & Contradictions

Analytical review of structural tensions identified in class notes

Analysis
Controversial Article 70

An MP loses their seat if they vote against their party. This creates a direct contradiction with Article 55(3) (collective cabinet responsibility to Parliament), preventing effective parliamentary control over the executive.

Religion vs Secularism Tension

Simultaneous retention of Bismillah-ar-Rahman-ar-Rahim and Islam as State Religion alongside Secularism (Art. 12) creates conceptual friction in the basic character of the state.

Prime Ministerial Power Imbalance

Lack of adequate checks and balances between the President and Prime Minister grants disproportionate executive power to the Prime Minister.

Articles Tensions & Politicization

Inter-article tensions among Articles 8, 27, 29, 55, and 70; dynamic politicization can hamper the democratic spirit if practiced partially.
